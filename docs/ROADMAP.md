# Hospital Simulator — Roadmap

> Milestones build on each other. Each milestone ends with something playable or testable. Content is added through `src/data/` rather than new code wherever possible.
> See [GAME_DESIGN.md](GAME_DESIGN.md) and [ARCHITECTURE.md](ARCHITECTURE.md).

## M0: Foundations

**Delivers:** Vite + TS + PixiJS + Preact project, git repo, lint/test setup, empty tile map with camera pan/zoom, fixed-timestep loop with pause and speed controls, seeded RNG, dev debug overlay.
**Done when:** `npm run dev` shows a pannable/zoomable grid, the speed controls change the tick rate, and `npm test` runs.

## M1: Build mode & saving

**Delivers:** place and remove walls, doors and floor. Zone rooms by painting, with flood-fill room detection and a validation checklist. Equipment catalogue (initial ~25 items) with ghost preview, rotation, cost deduction. Room capability derivation. Save/load to IndexedDB plus export/import.
**Done when:** you can build a small A&E (reception, waiting, triage, 2 minors cubicles, toilet), see valid/invalid room status, save, reload the browser and continue.
**Tests:** room detection, validation rules, save round-trip.

## M2: First patient flow (vertical slice)

**Delivers:** walk-in arrivals, reception booking, waiting, triage, minors treatment, discharge. Staff hiring (receptionist, nurse, junior doctor, cleaner), job board, A* movement, basic needs (seat, toilet), mood and LWBS. Tariff income, salaries, daily P&L. Notifications feed and inspector panel. 5 starter conditions.
**Done when:** a small A&E runs autonomously for an in-game day, patients flow end-to-end, money changes, and an understaffed or under-seated A&E visibly struggles.
**Tests:** headless 24-hour sim on a fixture layout, with invariants checked.
**As built (2026-10-08):** the five conditions are laceration, sprained ankle, minor head injury, abdominal pain and minor illness. A nurse triages every walk-in, then a free clinician calls the most urgent patient (earliest Manchester Triage deadline) to a free, clean cubicle; patients stay on the couch between steps. Cleaners wipe cubicles between patients and clean toilets. Emergency Nurse Practitioners (Band 7) see and treat minor injuries without a doctor; pathway steps list the roles allowed, in order of preference. Staff work around the clock until rotas arrive in M5, so staffing is cheaper than real. Deferred: X-rays and bloods (M4), staff rest and the staff room (M5), ambience and companions (M6/M7).

## M3: Majors, Resus, wards & monitoring

**Delivers:** Majors/Resus rooms, ambulance bays and ambulance arrivals with handover delays. Admissions to wards via porters and bed movement mode. Nurse stations, **line of sight**, central monitoring with response distance, coverage overlay. Deterioration, crash events and incident reports. Realistic death process (§5.6): expected vs unexpected deaths, verification, last offices, relatives' room, porter to mortuary, medical examiner and coroner, incident records, staff morale impact. 4-hour target tracking.
**Done when:** the coverage overlay correctly flags blind spots, and an unmonitored deteriorating patient produces an incident with a readable cause.
**Progress (2026-10-08), step 1 of 4: Majors and line of sight.** Built: the Nurse Station (a free staff nurse waits at it between jobs and watches every bed in sight within 12 tiles), line of sight (standard walls and doors block it, glazed walls don't), and the coverage overlay (**O** or the Coverage button; beds green, amber or red). Four majors conditions (chest pain, shortness of breath, suspected sepsis, a fall in an older person) need a trolley in a Majors Bay, get observations every 30 or 60 minutes with a NEWS2 score, and may deteriorate before treatment. Staff notice at NEWS2 5+ if they're with the patient, taking obs, or watching from a staffed station; that escalates the patient and buys an hour, and the condition's stabilising step ends the danger. Unnoticed or untreated, the patient has a cardiac arrest: a crash call pulls a doctor and a nurse off other work, and a patient safety incident lists the contributing factors (shown in Reports). Interim choices: majors patients only arrive once a Majors Bay works (until transfer-out in M4); every arrest ends in a transfer to intensive care until the death process (step 4); the "!" marker shows deterioration before staff notice.
**Step 2 of 4: ambulances and Resus (2026-10-08).** Built: the Ambulance Bay (zoned on foundations; each clear 3×6 space parks one ambulance), ambulance arrivals (about 22 a day at default volume, majors-heavy, plus two Resus conditions: anaphylaxis and septic shock), and handover. A nurse takes over each patient from the crew (handover includes triage): at a free trolley in Majors or Resus if they need one, or where they are if they can sit, after which they wait like a walk-in. With no free trolley, the crew waits with the patient (and keeps an eye on them); with no free space, later ambulances wait outside. The handover clock runs from arrival (target 15 minutes; Reports shows the average and how many took over 30 and 60). Patients now wait on their trolley when their next step is in another room, so Resus patients move on to Majors only when a bay is free. Septic shock ends in a transfer to intensive care. Interim choices: ambulances only come once a bay exists; diverting comes with department status (M5).
**Step 3 of 4: porters, wards, admissions and central monitoring (2026-10-08).** Built: Porters (a new role), the Ward room (beds and a sink), and admissions. After their last A&E step some patients are admitted (each condition has its own chance, length of stay and inpatient tariff). They wait on their A&E trolley until a porter takes them to a free, clean ward bed: wheeled on the trolley from Majors or Resus, which needs a bed-width route (double doors, everything 2 tiles wide), or walking from Minors. The A&E clock stops when they reach the ward; the decision-to-admit wait is tracked against 4 and 12 hours ("trolley waits"). After their stay, a doctor's discharge review sends them home and the inpatient tariff is paid. A full ward backs up into A&E (exit block), which holds trolleys and delays ambulance handovers. The Central Monitoring Station is a nurse station that also covers any bed with a bedside monitor within a 25 m walk (shown teal on the coverage overlay). Interim: a hospital with no ward sends patients who need admitting to another hospital; ward patients' needs (meals, toilets) and ward deterioration aren't modelled yet; all staff (including ward nurses and doctors) are one shared pool until departments and rotas (M5).
**Step 4 of 4: the death process and 4-hour tracking (2026-10-09).** Built (§5.6): a resuscitation now restarts the heart only sometimes (45% if the doctor-led team starts at once, 2% less for each minute before the team leader arrives, 10% when the hospital's arrest team has to take over); otherwise the patient dies and the team leader verifies it. A small share of ward admissions are end-of-life care: they're given a side room if one's free, and die expectedly on the ward (dying in an open bay brings a complaint). After every death: verification (a doctor or nurse), the family told by a doctor in the Relatives' Room (a complaint without one), last offices by two nurses with the bay closed, a porter taking them to the Mortuary on a covered trolley (a complaint if the route crosses a waiting area), a deep clean of the bay, Medical Examiner review at a desk, coroner referral for some unexpected deaths (holding them for days), then release to the funeral director. No mortuary space records an incident and keeps the bay closed; after 6 hours, a contingency arrangement takes them. Staff have morale: it drops for those involved in a death (most after a failed resuscitation), the resuscitation team has a short hot debrief, and it recovers slowly. The top bar shows today's 4-hour figure (green at 95%, amber at the 78% interim target), and Reports the last 7 days. New: Side Room, Relatives' Room, Mortuary (mortuary fridge units, three spaces each), and the Medical Examiner role. Interim: morale has no effects yet (fatigue, sickness and resignations come with M5); there's no PSIRF investigation, bereavement office or NHS Resolution claims yet; families aren't on the map (companions arrive in M6/M7).
**M3 is complete.**

## M3.5: Site access

**Delivers:** a council road with pavements across the map (a little south of the middle) and a bus stop. Walk-ins arrive from either end of the road or off a bus and walk along the pavement; ambulances drive in along it. The player connects the hospital with **footpaths** (cheap) and an **access road** (dearer). People can cut across grass at half speed; ambulances need tarmac, so an Ambulance Bay only works once a road links it to the public road. The Ambulance Bay can be zoned on access road as well as foundations. The road and pavements are council land: free, and never buildable. A per-tile land mask (owned/public) is the start of M7's purchasable parcels.
**Done when:** a new game starts on the road, walk-ins follow pavements and paths to the door, and an ambulance bay with no road to it gets no ambulances, with an alert saying why.
**Steps:** (1) grid surfaces, land ownership and the road on new maps; (2) footpath and access road build tools, ambulance bays on tarmac; (3) walking: grass cost and speed, arrivals at the road ends and bus stop; (4) ambulances driving, with road connectivity and 3-wide clearance; (5) road rendering (kerbs, markings, bus stop, public-land tint); (6) old saves get a road and a free path; (7) fixtures and tests; (8) docs.
**Step 1 (2026-10-09).** Built: `Path` and `Road` surfaces, the `land` mask (saved; save v9), and `layOutSite()` (world/site.ts), which new games call (`createSimState({ site: true })`; blank test maps leave it off). Foundations, walls and floor removal skip or refuse council land, and the camera starts just north of the bus stop. Interim until later steps: arrivals still use the nearest map edge, pre-v9 saves have no road, and the road is drawn as plain fills.
**Step 2 (2026-10-09).** Built: the `pave` command and the Footpath (£40/tile) and Access road (£150/tile) tools under Construction → Outside. Paving goes on grass or other paving (never over foundations or council land); swapping one surface for another, laying foundations or walls over paving, or removing it refunds half the old surface. The Ambulance Bay (and only it, via the room's `onRoad` flag) can be zoned on access road, and paving now counts as somewhere to stand, so a bay on tarmac parks ambulances. Interim: any bay works whether or not a road links it to the public road (step 4).
**Step 3 (2026-10-09).** Built: grass costs double in route finding and is walked at half speed, so people keep to pavements and paths. On a map with the road, walk-ins arrive at either end of the hospital-side pavement or at the bus stop (20% by bus); people leave, and new staff arrive, at the arrival point nearest A&E reception. Maps without a road keep the nearest-edge behaviour. Three seed-sensitive tests were made robust (the ward test now uses a seed whose first admissions include short stays).
**Step 4 (2026-10-09).** Built: vehicles drive on tarmac and Ambulance Bays, clear of walls, doors and items, wherever there's a clear 3×3 square (world/vehicles.ts). An Ambulance Bay space only counts if a vehicle can reach it from the public road; otherwise ambulances go elsewhere, with an alert. The Access road tool can cross the council pavement as a dropped kerb (it stays council land). On a map with the road, an ambulance comes in at either end, keeps left (driving against the traffic costs extra in route finding), drives to its space (about 2 tiles a tick), parks, and drives off after turnaround. With the bay full, it queues on its side of the road short of the turn-off. The handover clock still starts when it comes onto the map. Maps without a road keep the instant parking. Save v10 stores each ambulance's position, route and phase.
**Step 5 (2026-10-09).** Built: kerbstones wherever paving meets a carriageway, a dashed centre line between the lanes, dropped kerbs in lighter tarmac, player footpaths in warmer block paving than the council pavement, yellow box markings round Ambulance Bays (in place of the zone tint on tarmac), a bus stop (flag with a bus on it, and a glazed shelter), and a red wash with a dashed edge over council land while a build tool is selected.
**Step 6 (2026-10-09).** Built: saves with no road (any version before 11) get one when loaded (save v11), fitted just south of everything built with a two-row grass gap, plus a free footpath from the southernmost outside door down to the pavement and the bus stop beside it (`fitSite()` in world/site.ts). Ambulances waiting outside move onto the road. If the road won't fit below the building, or the map is too small, the save keeps map-edge arrivals. Old Ambulance Bays aren't linked to the road automatically: the alert tells the player to add an access road. The base64 helpers moved to save/base64.ts so migrations can decode grids.
**Step 7 (2026-10-09).** Built: the small and majors fixtures take an opt-in `site: true`, which fits the road in below the building (as for an old save) with a footpath to the front door, and for majors with an ambulance bay, an access road down to the road. tests/sim/siteFlow.test.ts runs a whole day of each: walk-ins all arrive at the road ends or bus stop, patients outside stay off the grass, and ambulances drive in, hand over and drive off without leaving tarmac. Changed from the plan: the existing fixtures stay roadless, since they test clinical logic and moving them would reshuffle every seeded outcome without testing anything new. Across five seeds the small A&E's 4-hour figure is in the same range with the road (50–74%) as without (46–84%).
**Step 8 (2026-10-09).** ARCHITECTURE (module layout, land, vehicle routing, migrations that add content) and GAME_DESIGN (exterior elements, arrival channels) updated for the road.
**M3.5 is complete.**

Small fixes and polish from playtesting, to slot in between milestones.

- ~~**Dirty bays and areas**~~ (done 2026-10-09): brown smudges and a brown badge on a couch, trolley, bed or toilet waiting to be cleaned (including the deep clean after a death), a blue sparkle while a cleaner works on it, and a red badge on a toilet too dirty to use (render/cleaningLayer.ts). The item's hover card says what it's waiting for, e.g. "no cleaner on staff".
- ~~**Unreachable rooms for patients**~~ (done 2026-10-09): after each layout change, a flood from the entrance over tiles any patient may cross finds rooms patients walk into (reception, waiting, toilets, wards and pathway rooms) that they can only reach through another clinical room (`roomsPatientsCantReach` in places.ts). Each gets an alert that jumps to it, and an amber line in its inspector and hover card.
- ~~**Where idle staff wait**~~ (done 2026-10-09): staff no longer stand where their last job ended. Each role waits at a base in its own department (systems/bases.ts): doctors and nurse practitioners at the new A&E Staff Base (a desk), registrars and consultants on their specialty's ward, radiographers in X-ray or CT, biomedical scientists in the lab, porters in the new Porters' Lodge (a desk) and cleaners in the new Domestic Services Room (a sink). Nurses still wait at a nurse station, using the Staff Base when every station is taken. They sit behind a free desk or on a free seat (sofas, benches, chairs), and stand once those are taken, moving to a seat when one frees up. Without their base (or when it's full, one tile each), staff wait in the Staff Room, and with no Staff Room they stay put. The Staff Room is still meant for breaks once fatigue arrives (M5). The Help panel gains a "Staff areas" checklist. No save change: the new rooms are content.
- **Exit block from a small ward** (budget check, 2026-10-09): a 6-bed ward fills within a day (stays of 2–8 days), and admitted patients then wait indefinitely in Majors Bays and Minors cubicles. In the majors fixture that blocked walk-ins (about 30 a day left unseen, ambulances diverted 10 a day, 4-hour figure about 13%), so adding a small ward did worse than having none, where admissions are transferred out. Real exit block is right to model, but: admitted patients shouldn't hold a Minors cubicle (move them to a Majors trolley or a corridor space), there should be an escalation such as transfer after a long trolley wait, and the starting budget should allow a ward that's viable at the default catchment (25–30 beds).
- **Toilets beyond the waiting area**: only patients in the A&E waiting area go to the toilet; in a cubicle, on a trolley awaiting a bed, or on a ward the need is never met (ward patients' bladders freeze on arrival, and since 2026-10-09 their toilet need is simply hidden). Make it real: ward patients' needs keep rising; those who can walk use a toilet in or near the ward, and those who can't (trolley/bed-bound, end of life) get a nurse with a commode or bedpan (a short nursing job, privacy curtain, sluice visit). Patients in A&E cubicles or awaiting a bed ask a nurse rather than wandering off. Fits with ward care (meals, washing) alongside M4/M5.

## M4: Outpatients, theatres & specialty gating

**Delivers:** specialties, consultants (resident and on-call with callout delay), outpatient clinics with GP referrals and scheduled appointments, waiting list and RTT, theatres + recovery + elective surgery scheduling and cancellations, imaging (X-ray/CT) and lab. **Specialty gating** of referrals, plus A&E transfer-out when the hospital can't treat. **Medical Examiner as a consultant duty** (GAME_DESIGN §5.6, §6.1): replaces the interim dedicated Medical Examiner role from M3. The player assigns the duty to consultants, who do reviews in set office-hours sessions and clinical work otherwise, and never review a death of a patient they treated. **Shared mobile equipment:** some kit is a wheeled machine shared across a department rather than one per bay, as in a real ED. A 12-lead ECG (and later portable X-ray and ultrasound) serves any Majors Bay within a walking distance of where it's kept, like the central monitor's response distance, with staff fetching it and only one patient using it at a time. Replaces the interim rule that each bay needs its own ECG.
**Done when:** adding a cardiology clinic and consultant causes cardiology referrals to start, and removing them stops them.
**Steps:** (1) consultants, specialties and on-call: Consultant and Registrar roles tagged with a specialty (General Medicine, Cardiology, General Surgery, Trauma & Orthopaedics, Anaesthetics), resident or on-call (off site until called, callout delay and fee), a specialty review step in A&E pathways before admission, admissions to a ward of the right specialty; (2) Medical Examiner as a consultant duty (office-hours sessions, never their own patients), retiring the interim role, with old saves converted; (3) specialty gating and A&E transfer-out: conditions name the specialty they need, and A&E stabilises and transfers out what the hospital can't treat (cost, reputation, the 4-hour clock runs until they leave), replacing the interim majors-only-with-a-Majors-Bay and no-ward rules; (4) imaging and lab: X-ray, CT (shielded walls) and Lab rooms, Radiographer and Lab Technician roles, porters carrying samples, result turnaround, X-ray for sprains (some are fractures), troponin for chest pain, CT for head injuries under NICE rules, door-to-CT time; (5) shared mobile equipment: a wheeled 12-lead ECG and portable X-ray serving bays within a walking distance, fetched by staff, one patient at a time; (6) outpatients: Outpatient Clinic Room, consultant clinic sessions, GP referrals gated by specialty, first and follow-up appointments, DNAs, the waiting list and 18-week RTT clock, outpatient tariff (meets "done when"); (7) theatres and elective surgery: Theatre and Recovery, decision to operate in clinic, admitted waiting list and theatre lists, on-the-day cancellation with no bed (rebook within 28 days), emergency surgery from A&E. Each step bumps the save version with fixtures and tests; docs and a balance pass at the end.
**Interim (until M5 rotas):** resident consultants work 24/7 like other staff, but are unavailable while running a clinic or theatre session.
**Step 1 (2026-10-09): consultants, specialties and on-call.** Built: five specialties (data/specialties.ts) and two specialist roles hired into one, the Consultant (£160k a year resident) and the Registrar (£88k, resident only). An on-call consultant stays at home off the map, costing an £8k availability supplement plus £80 an hour while in the hospital (in place of the per-callout fee first planned: it's how the NHS consultant contract pays on-call). They're called in when a job for their specialty is waiting and nobody who could do it is in the hospital, arrive 20 to 40 minutes later, and go home after an hour with nothing for them. Each condition that can be admitted names its specialty (chest pain: Cardiology; abdominal pain and head injury: General Surgery; breathing, sepsis, falls and anaphylaxis: General Medicine). A patient who needs admitting is now referred: they stay on their trolley or couch (the 4-hour clock still running) until a registrar or consultant of that specialty reviews them (20 to 40 minutes) and decides to admit. A pathway step can be a referral too: septic shock's critical care referral goes to Anaesthetics. Wards can be given to a specialty in the inspector: patients go to their own specialty's ward, then one open to any, then another specialty's (an outlier). Reports shows specialty reviews, the average referral-to-decision time and outliers. Save v13. Interim: with no team for a specialty, A&E admits its patients itself, with an alert (A&E transfers out in step 3); ward discharge reviews are still done by junior doctors; consultants don't yet do clinics, theatre or ward rounds.
**Step 2 (2026-10-09): the Medical Examiner as a consultant duty.** Built: a resident consultant can be given the Medical Examiner duty (a checkbox in their inspector). They review deaths at a desk in sessions, Monday to Friday 09:00 to 17:00, doing clinical work the rest of the time, and never review a death of a patient they treated (each patient records the consultants who worked with them). The game clock now has days of the week (day 1 is a Monday), so a death on a Friday evening waits in the mortuary until Monday morning, and the death checklist says when the next session is. The dedicated Medical Examiner role is gone: old saves (v14) turn each one into a General Medicine consultant with the duty, which costs more (£160k rather than £42k) but also gives the hospital a medical team. Interim: on-call consultants can't take the duty; the session is the same for every Medical Examiner (no job plans until M5).
**Step 3 (2026-10-09): specialty gating and A&E transfer-out.** Built (systems/transfers.ts): a patient whose next step has nowhere in the hospital it could happen is transferred once their step has waited 30 minutes; so is one who needs admitting when there's no ward, or no consultant or registrar of their specialty (replacing step 1's interim, where A&E admitted them itself, and M3's instant transfer when there was no ward). A doctor (junior, registrar or consultant) assesses them and arranges the transfer (15 to 30 minutes, urgent if they're escalated), then they wait where they are, on their trolley or in the waiting area, for a transfer ambulance: 30 to 90 minutes for triage categories 1 and 2, otherwise 1 to 4 hours. Each costs £400 (a new "Transfer ambulances" line in Finance). The 4-hour clock runs until they leave; Reports shows transfers and the average wait from decision to departure. Majors walk-ins now come to a hospital without a Majors Bay: a quarter of walk-ins it can't treat turn up anyway, while ambulance control still only brings what it can treat. Save v15. Interim: no reputation cost yet (M6); septic shock still goes straight to intensive care (an ICU of your own comes later); GP referrals, the other half of specialty gating, come with outpatients (step 6).
**Step 4 (2026-10-09): imaging and lab.** Built (systems/diagnostics.ts): the lead-lined wall (£1,400/tile), and three rooms in a new Diagnostics section: the X-ray Room and CT Room (each must be enclosed by lead-lined walls) and the Pathology Lab, with an X-ray unit (£120k), CT scanner (£650k) and blood analyser (£80k). Two roles: the Radiographer (£49.5k) and the Biomedical Scientist (£40.5k). Pathway steps can take a blood sample, be a scan, wait for results, or happen for only some patients. A porter takes each sample to the lab; a biomedical scientist processes it (5 to 10 minutes) and results follow after the analyser's 30 to 45 minutes. Walking patients go to X-ray and back to wait; Majors and Resus patients keep their trolley while scanned. CT is reported 20 to 45 minutes after the scan. Conditions now use them: ankle X-rays for about a third of sprains (Ottawa rules) and every broken ankle (a new condition, admitted to Trauma & Orthopaedics if unstable); CT head for a quarter of head injuries and 30% of falls (NICE); bloods for chest pain (troponin), abdominal pain, sepsis, breathlessness and falls; a chest X-ray for 80% of breathless patients. Without a lab, patients needing bloods are transferred. Reports gains a Diagnostics section (X-rays, CT scans, arrival to CT, CT within an hour of request, blood turnaround), and the Help panel a Diagnostics checklist. Save v16. Test fixtures gained a diagnostics block east of the building (reached through a door in the waiting area's north wall and a short walk outside) and a radiographer, biomedical scientist and porter; they now start with £5M. Balance to watch: results waits hold cubicles and trolleys, so the majors fixture's 4-hour figure fell from 50–84% to 25–71% across five seeds, and a full diagnostics set costs about £1m of the £1.5m starting cash. Interim: patients walk to scans (a porter and nurse escort for trolley patients come with shared equipment in step 5, alongside portable X-ray); radiologists aren't hired staff.
**Step 5 (2026-10-09), part 1: shared ECG and portable X-ray.** Built (world/sharedKit.ts): equipment can be `mobile`, wheeled kit shared across a department. Each item lends its capabilities to every room within 25 m of walking from where it's kept (worked out when rooms are detected, so the room checks, "No Majors Bay with …" messages and the Help panel all count it, labelled "within 25 m"). A step that needs shared kit reserves the nearest free item that reaches the bed; whoever takes the job fetches it, uses it at the bedside, and wheels it back as a job of their own. One patient at a time, and the kit is drawn beside them while it's out, with a faint outline where it's kept. The 12-lead ECG is now shared: ECGs already in bays carry on working as shared ones. New: the Portable X-ray (£95k, £800 a month). A radiographer takes it to Majors and Resus patients who shouldn't leave their monitoring (anyone in Resus, category 1, deteriorating, or on continuous monitoring), so breathless and septic patients are X-rayed on their trolley; everyone else goes to the X-ray Room. Suspected sepsis gained a chest X-ray for 60% of patients (NICE NG51, looking for a chest source). Save v21. Interim: kit that's interrupted mid-use (a crash call, a dismissal) is back where it's kept at once; only the ECG and X-ray are shared so far (ultrasound later).
**Step 5 (2026-10-09), part 2: escorts to scans.** Built (systems/escorts.ts): a patient on a Majors or Resus trolley called for X-ray or CT is wheeled there on it by a porter, and back once the scan's done (an "escort" job each way; the trolley stays theirs meanwhile). One who shouldn't leave their monitoring (the portable X-ray's rule: Resus, category 1, deteriorating, or continuous monitoring) also has a nurse, who comes before the porter sets off and stays through the scan until they're back. Until collected they wait where they are: the scanner is held for them before, and stays taken after, so a porter shortage now slows scanning. Without porters, an alert says trolley patients are waiting for a scan. Patients' and staff's panels say who's coming and where they're going; the patient is drawn on their trolley with the porter pushing. Routes need bed width like ward transfers; without one a porter squeezes them through on foot, with a warning (the test fixture's diagnostics block has single doors, so its trips do this). No save change (a new job kind only). Interim: the escort nurse doesn't do observations that fall due during the trip (another nurse comes); Resus patients don't take a doctor along. **Step 5 is complete.**
**Step 6 (2026-10-10): outpatients.** Decisions (user, 2026-10-10): a real 18-week clock, with waiting lists kept as records rather than people; three outpatient-led specialties added (Dermatology, ENT, Ophthalmology); clinics scheduled by giving clinic rooms a specialty and each doctor a number of weekly sessions (a job plan), rather than a timetable grid. Built in three parts. (1) Content: the specialties and each one's outpatient service (referral rate, urgent share, slot lengths, follow-up and decision-to-operate chances, first and follow-up tariffs), the Outpatients Reception and Outpatient Clinic Room (desk, couch, sink; 3×4 minimum, the NHS 16 m² consulting room), a specialty for clinic rooms like wards (the setting is now roomSpecialties), and 0–6 clinic sessions a week in the inspector of resident consultants and registrars (systems/clinics.ts). Save v22. (2) systems/referrals.ts and systems/outpatients.ts: GP referrals (gated on reception, a clinic room and a consultant with sessions, which meets "done when"), the waiting list, booking up to six weeks ahead, did-not-attends, hospital cancellations, clinic days with patients booking in, waiting and being called in, clinicians off the job board in session (and not counted as available when deciding to call in an on-call consultant), outcomes, tariff on its own income line. Save v23. (3) Reports (an 18-week tile, an outpatients day section, a waiting-lists table, the income split), the Help panel's Outpatients checklist and clinic sessions needed for each specialty's demand, alerts (no receptionist on the Outpatients desk, a list whose clinics have stopped, sessions with no room). Balance (80k catchment, 8 weeks): a cardiology consultant with 4 sessions sees about 12 new patients a week, keeping up with demand; income starts at about £2.7k a week against £3.7k for the consultant and a receptionist, and should reach about £5k as follow-ups come due (6–12 weeks), so a clinic pays its way modestly, like a real one. Interim: patients listed for surgery wait with nothing to serve them until theatres (step 7); outpatients don't need X-rays or bloods yet; no A&E-to-fracture-clinic referrals; outpatients don't use toilets or get impatient; only consultants open a specialty to referrals (registrars add sessions).
**Step 7 (2026-10-10): theatres and elective surgery.** Decisions (user, 2026-10-10): a Theatre Practitioner role (two per operation, a staff nurse in Recovery); emergencies bump planned operations and bring in the on-call team out of hours, with theatres that can be kept for emergencies; day cases go home the same day while inpatients need a ward bed or are cancelled on the day. **Step 1 (2026-10-10): shifts and rotas.** Built (systems/rota.ts): staff now work NHS long days (07:30–20:00) and nights (19:30–08:00), three 12½-hour shifts a week, with half an hour where the two shifts overlap for handover. The Hire panel has a Rota section with cover levels for each rota group (each role, and registrars by specialty): how many you want on every day and every night shift. Everyone hired is rostered onto a weekly rota that meets the cover as far as it can (the shift furthest short first, never a day shift straight after a night, nor a day and night on the same date); shifts left over go where they keep the day:night split closest to the cover's (60:40 with none set). The panel shows how many are rostered by day and night, how many are at work now, and the shifts a week left short with the headcount the cover needs. At each shift change the incoming staff walk in from the site entrance and the outgoing ones finish what they're doing, then go home; off-duty staff are off the map and take no jobs. Someone hired mid-shift starts at once only if the rota puts them on it and their group is short right now; otherwise at their first shift (the joining message says when). Resident consultants work weekdays 08:00–18:00; out of hours one consultant of each specialty is on call from home (weeknights in turn, a whole weekend from Friday evening at a time) and called in like an on-call consultant. Registrars with clinics or theatre lists work weekdays too (without the on call), so their sessions are always covered. Unsocial hours pay (Agenda for Change bands 3+: 30% between 20:00 and 06:00 and on Saturdays, 60% on Sundays) is added to salaries for every hour someone works on a shift. The inspector shows each person's pattern and next shift; the People dialog says who's at home; a shift that starts short of its cover raises an alert; the Help panel's capacity guide now gives people to hire (people needed at once × 14 ÷ 3). Staff gained `contract` (resident or on call; `onCall` is now the call-out state of anyone on call from home), `duty` (on, leaving, off) and `shiftEnd`. Save v27: old hospitals were staffed for everyone working around the clock, so their rotas start off, with a one-way "Start shifts and rotas" button in Settings; test fixtures also run without rotas unless `state.settings.rotas` is set. Interim: cover is hospital-wide per role, not per department; fatigue, bank and agency come in step 2; the economy isn't rebalanced until step 3, and keeping a post filled day and night now costs about five salaries rather than one, so the 2026-10-09 budget check's surpluses no longer hold.
**Step 2 (2026-10-10): staff depth.** Built (systems/workforce.ts): nurses are hired at a band (one Nurse role with `band`: Band 5 Staff Nurse £40.5k, Band 6 Senior Staff Nurse £49.5k, Band 7 Charge Nurse £61k, with on-costs), each band its own rota group ("nurse:band5"), so cover can ask for a Band 7 on every shift. While a Band 7 is on duty the shift has a nurse in charge and nurses work 10% faster (one is enough). Every member of staff has skill (0–100, random within their band's or role's range at hire, growing by 0.02 an hour worked to 95): work speed runs from 0.85× at 0 to 1.15× at 100, applied as each tick's progress on a job. Fatigue builds by 3 an hour on site plus 2 while busy and falls by 6 an hour at home (only with rotas on); above 50 it slows people, down to 0.8× at 100, and above 75 it costs morale. Morale drifts back to 75 by half a point an hour; at the end of each shift a busy one (over 80% of it on jobs, clinics or theatre) costs 3, starting short of cover 2 more, and a quiet one (under 40%) gives 1; below 40 it slows people slightly. Sickness: each shift (or weekday, for doctors working weekdays) someone may call in sick, 4.5% of the time, up to 3× as morale falls below 50, and 1.5× when tired. Resignations: once a day, someone below 30 morale may hand in their notice (up to 12% a day at 0) and leaves four weeks later, once off shift. Rota gaps: at the start of each shift, each group's places short of its cover (not rostered, or off sick) are offered to the staff bank, who fill about two in three at normal hourly pay, and then, if the policy allows, to an agency, who always find someone at twice the cost, a little less skilled and 10% slower as they don't know the hospital. They're temporary staff (contract "bank" or "agency") who come for the shift and are gone after it; a notification lists each shift's gaps and how they were filled. The policy (leave unfilled, bank only, bank then agency; bank only by default) is in the Hire panel's Rota section, the first of the hospital policies (`state.policies`). Bank and agency pay have their own lines in Finance. The inspector shows skill and fatigue with morale, and says if someone is off sick or working their notice; the Help checklist suggests a Band 7. **Step 3 (2026-10-10): NHS funding and the economy rebalance.** Decision (user, 2026-10-10): emergency income as a block contract plus a marginal rate, like the NHS payment scheme's aligned payment and incentive, rather than a readiness fee with full tariff or a lagged block. Built (systems/funding.ts): the block is 70% of the expected full-tariff value of the emergency care the hospital is equipped to provide for its catchment. That's the attendances it can treat, as far as its rooms have time for them (each condition by its busiest room, a patient holding a room 3× their treatment time at 85% use), plus emergency admissions and operations, as far as ward beds can take them at the average stay (only with a ward and that specialty's own consultant or registrar). It's capped at 120% of the emergency care actually delivered over the last 28 days, once there's a week of it, so unstaffed beds and patients who leave unseen aren't paid for. It's paid as a daily share at midnight ("due at midnight" in Reports until then). Each emergency patient earns 30% of their tariff (A&E attendance, emergency admission and emergency operation); planned operations are paid in full on a new "Planned operations" line. Two running costs fill out the books: drugs and clinical supplies (18% of each treated episode's full tariff, 5% for outpatient appointments) and estates and utilities (£270 a year per floor tile, about £200/m², with a tile about 1.33 m²). The game now has a calendar: day 1 is Monday 1 April, the start of the NHS financial year, and the clock shows the date ("Mon 1 Apr"). At the end of each month a statement is filed (`state.month` accumulates filed days, `state.months` keeps 24) and a notification gives income, costs, the surplus or deficit and cash. Reports gains a Monthly statement table (this month so far beside last month, line by line, with building and equipment spending kept apart from running costs, and cash at the end), and the Help panel a "Paying your way" section. Starting cash rose from £1.5M to £3.5M.
